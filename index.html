What is a Geometric Foundation Model?

A Geometric Foundation Model (GFM), such as DA3 or VGGT, is a feed-forward transformer designed to map one or more RGB images to dense 3D geometry, outputting per-pixel depth or 3D point maps. Unlike standard vision-language backbones that discard explicit spatial configurations, a GFM preserves dense structural representations across multiple intermediate hidden states, offering a rich perceptual prior for contact-rich robot manipulation.

Repurposing GFM Beyond Static Feature Extraction

Standard Paradigms

Vision-Language-Action (VLA)* models ground natural language instructions in 2D visual representations but struggle with 3D viewpoint variations. Video World-Action Models (WAM)* employ large video diffusion substrates to capture temporal world dynamics, yet their latent predictions remain bound to 2D pixel space.

The GAM Paradigm Instead of treating the GFM as a frozen, passive feature extractor via alignment or distillation, Geometric Action Model (GAM) explicitly overrides the GFM's deep layers to serve as the policy's own temporal prediction and action decoding engine.

METHOD

Causal Feature Prediction

GAM splits the GFM backbone at an intermediate layer LsL_sLs​. The shallow layers function as an independent observation encoder. At the split layer, a causal transformer layer forecasts future latent tokens conditioned on language instructions, proprioception, and previous actions. Crucially, the single action token is propagated through the remaining deep GFM blocks alongside predicted future geometric features, enabling joint generation of downstream depth maps and final robot action commands.

ABSTRACT

Generalist robot policies must follow user instructions while reasoning about how objects, cameras, and robot actions interact in the 3D physical world. Recent vision-language-action models and video world-action models inherit strong semantic or temporal priors from large-scale foundation models, but they still operate primarily on 2D image frames or 2D-derived latent spaces, leaving the geometry required for contact-rich manipulation implicit.

We propose Geometric Action Model (GAM), a language-conditioned manipulation policy that directly repurposes a pretrained Geometric Foundation Model (GFM) as a shared substrate for perception, temporal prediction, and action decoding. GAM splits the GFM at an intermediate layer, where the shallow layers serve as an observation encoder, while a causal future predictor is inserted at the split layer to forecast future latent tokens conditioned on language, proprioception, and action history. The predicted future tokens are then routed through the remaining GFM blocks during feature propagation and decoding, allowing a single backbone to produce both future geometry and actions.

This design equips the GFM with language-conditioned temporal world modeling using only minimal architectural modification, while preserving its rich geometric priors. On a broad suite of simulation and real-robot manipulation benchmarks, GAM is more accurate, more robust, faster, and lighter than current foundation-model-scale baselines.
